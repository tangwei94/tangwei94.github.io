Research

Research directions and selected projects.

1. Infinite projected entangled-pair states

Infinite projected entangled-pair states (iPEPS) are now among the most important numerical methods for studying strongly interacting quantum lattice models in two dimensions. Our recent work focuses on improving their efficiency, stability, and scope of application.

One of the central bottlenecks in iPEPS simulations is the high computational cost of variational optimization. To mitigate this issue, we introduced a preconditioning scheme that incorporates the geometry of the iPEPS manifold to substantially accelerate convergence with negligible additional cost [1].

Another major challenge in iPEPS simulations is that approximate environment contractions break the exact variational principle, allowing the optimizer to exploit contraction errors and become unstable. We identified that the gauge degrees of freedom on the virtual indices play an important role in this instability by strongly affecting the accuracy of two-dimensional tensor network contractions [2]. Building on this insight, we developed a manifold-based gauge-fixed optimization scheme that maintains the iPEPS in the minimal canonical form and significantly improves optimization stability [3].

We have also extended the iPEPS framework to two-dimensional interacting systems in a uniform magnetic field, where conventional approaches require large magnetic unit cells. To bypass this limitation, we introduced a gauge-covariant iPEPS ansatz in which physical observables remain translation invariant and the magnetic flux per plaquette enters as a continuous parameter [4].

2. Continuous tensor networks

In continuum quantum systems such as ultracold atomic gases and $(1+1)$-dimensional quantum field theories, the spatial degrees of freedom are intrinsically continuous. Continuous matrix product states (cMPS) provide a variational wave function ansatz that operates directly in the continuum limit without spatial discretization. To improve the efficiency of cMPS calculations, we employed Riemannian optimization and preconditioning techniques, enabling accurate studies of emergent Kac-Moody symmetries in one-dimensional bosonic systems [1]. We further extended this framework to multi-component cMPS, providing an effective numerical tool for quantum mixtures such as multi-species bosonic gases [2].

Continuous tensor networks also arise naturally in quantum lattice models at finite temperatures, where the path-integral representation of the partition function is continuous along the imaginary-time direction. We proposed the continuous matrix product operator (cMPO) approach to finite-temperature quantum states by taking the continuous-time limit of the partition-function tensor network, where the dominant eigenvector of the cMPO transfer matrix is obtained by variationally optimizing a uniform cMPS [3]. This method provides direct access to the thermodynamics of one- and quasi-one-dimensional quantum systems and has been applied to field-theoretic problems such as the $(1+1)$-dimensional $O(3)$ nonlinear $\sigma$-model with a $\theta=\pi$ topological term [4].

3. Tensor networks for disordered systems

Disorder plays a central role in quantum many-body physics, giving rise to phenomena such as localization, glassy dynamics, and infinite-randomness criticality. Despite its importance, numerical simulations of disordered quantum systems remain challenging. Conventional approaches typically rely on explicit sampling over many disorder realizations in finite-size systems, which is computationally expensive and becomes less reliable near disorder-dominated critical points due to rare-region effects and broad probability distributions.

Recently, we developed a tensor network method that computes disorder-averaged expectation values in random spin chains without explicit disorder sampling [1]. By exploiting statistical translational invariance, the algorithm operates directly in the thermodynamic limit using translationally invariant tensor networks. We demonstrated the effectiveness of this approach on random spin chains, including the infinite-randomness critical point of the random transverse-field Ising model, opening a new tensor network route to disordered quantum matter.

References

  • [1] K. Vervoort, W. Tang, and N. Bultinck, Extracting average properties of disordered spin chains with translationally invariant tensor networks, arXiv:2504.21089 (2025).

4. Klein bottle entropy in conformal critical systems

Conformal field theories (CFTs) govern critical points in one-dimensional quantum systems and two-dimensional classical statistical models. Because the central charge alone is often insufficient to uniquely identify the underlying CFT, additional universal quantities are needed to distinguish different critical theories. In particular, CFTs defined on the Klein bottle manifold exhibit a universal Klein bottle entropy, which together with the central charge provides a sharp fingerprint of conformal criticality.

In a series of works, we developed both the theoretical understanding and numerical methods for Klein bottle entropy in critical lattice models. We showed that the Klein bottle entropy can be transformed into a boundary entropy via a cut-and-sew construction, enabling efficient and accurate computation in lattice models with quantum Monte Carlo and tensor network methods [1]. Going beyond rational CFTs, we proved that the Klein bottle entropy of compactified boson CFTs directly yields the compactification radius, offering a practical route to extracting the Luttinger parameter in Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids [2]. More recently, we established the universal scaling of Klein bottle entropy near conformal critical points [3] and demonstrated that the Luttinger parameter can be extracted from a single wave function through its overlap with a crosscap state [4].
